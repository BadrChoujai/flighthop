What this actually does

Airlines sell you routes they have already decided exist. Ryanair flies from Vienna, and it flies to Tangier, but it does not sell Vienna → Tangier — so searching for it returns nothing.

Flighthop reads the network as a map rather than a list. It finds every airport the airline flies to from where you are and flies from to where you want to be, then pairs up flights whose timings actually work and prices the pair.

The catch, stated plainly: these are two separate tickets. The airline sells them as unrelated journeys and treats them that way. If the first flight is late and you miss the second, nobody owes you a seat, a refund, or a hotel. That risk is why these trips are cheap — and it is what the tags below measure.

What the tags mean

Every connection gets one tag, based on how long you have between landing and taking off again. The thresholds are deliberately more cautious than an airline's own minimum connection time, because on a single ticket the airline re-books you and here nobody does.

comfortable
4 hours 30 minutes or more

Time to land, wait for a bag, walk to departures, queue at check-in, clear security again, and still sit down before boarding. An hour or two of delay on the first flight does not cost you the second.

tight
3 to 4 hours 30 minutes

Workable, and usually fine — but it assumes nothing goes wrong. No long queue at passport control, no bag that takes forty minutes on the belt, no gate at the far end of the terminal. Reasonable with cabin baggage. Less so with a checked bag.

risky
Under 3 hours

One thing going wrong loses you the second flight, and the money you paid for it. Shown because it may be exactly what you want on a cabin-bag-only trip you could afford to redo — not because it is advisable. Check whether a later flight exists before booking one; the trip detail says so explicitly.

direct
No connection at all

One flight, one ticket, none of the above applies. When a direct flight exists it is shown alongside the connections, so you can see what the saving is actually worth.

The other things we flag

Open any trip and these appear underneath, where they apply.

Collect and re-check bags between flights
On every connection, because on separate tickets it is always true. Your bag is checked to the connecting airport, not your destination. You collect it, leave the secure area, and check in again from scratch.
Overnight connection
The second flight leaves before 06:00, the first lands after 23:00, or the two fall on different days. You will need somewhere to sleep, and some airports close landside overnight. Budget for a room before comparing this against a direct flight.
You clear immigration into [country]
A self-transfer always means formally entering the connecting country — there is no sterile transit corridor when you have to collect a bag. Usually routine. Worth reading if your passport needs a visa for that country.
Requires entry to [country] — outside the Schengen area you started in
The connection crosses a border your trip did not otherwise require. Vienna to Morocco via Italy stays inside Schengen until the final flight; the same trip via London means a UK entry, with whatever that involves for your nationality.
Only [n] hours to make the transfer · [n] hours waiting
The two ends of uncomfortable. Both feed the ranking, and both are adjustable.

Why the cheapest trip is not always first

A €35 fare with a seven-hour wait and an 05:45 alarm is not obviously better than €42 with a three-hour wait, so sorting purely by price answers the wrong question. Flighthop converts discomfort into money and adds it to the fare:

fare
+ €25 per hour   the layover falls short of 3h    (risk of missing it)
+ €8  per hour   it drags on past 8h              (dead time)
+ €60            overnight connection
+ €30            a border crossing the trip did not already require

That total is what Best sorts by. The two per-hour figures are the sidebar sliders — cost of a rushed transfer and cost of dead time. Set both to zero and Best becomes Cheapest. Push the first one up and short layovers sink. They are your numbers, not ours.

Cheapest and Fastest ignore the scoring entirely and sort by fare and by door-to-door time.

Questions

Can I book here?

No, and deliberately not. Each leg links out to Ryanair's own booking page and you buy them separately. Two tickets means two transactions, and hiding that behind a single "Book" button would be a lie with a real cost attached.

What happens if I miss the connection?

You have lost that ticket. No protection, no re-booking, no compensation — as far as the airline is concerned they are two unrelated journeys. Open a trip and Flighthop tells you whether a later flight exists on that route the same day, read from the published timetable. That is your real fallback, and it costs a new ticket.

Do I have to collect my bags at the connection?

Yes, on every connection here. Bags are checked to the connecting airport only. Factor in the belt, the walk, and a check-in desk that may not open until two hours before departure — that last one is why very short layovers fail even when both flights are on time.

Are the prices what I will actually pay?

They are the fare for one adult with a cabin bag, at the moment we asked. Checked bags, seat selection and priority are not included, and fares move — sometimes within the hour. Treat every number here as "worth looking at", then confirm on the airline's page before committing.

Why does one flight seem to arrive before it departs?

Because it does, on the clock. Madrid 13:10 → Tangier 12:40 is correct: the two are an hour apart, and Morocco does not follow the EU's daylight saving changes. Every time shown is local to that airport, which is what your boarding pass will say. The layover and total journey time are computed in UTC underneath, so they stay right across timezone changes.

Why is the minimum layover 2 hours?

Below that a self-transfer stops being realistic — you cannot reliably land, collect a bag, check in again and clear security in less. The slider goes down to 1 hour if you disagree and are carrying nothing but a cabin bag, but the floor exists because showing 40-minute connections would mean showing trips that do not work.

Why do some searches show fewer connections than others?

Two filters run before pricing. Connections through wildly out-of-the-way airports are dropped — the network technically allows Vienna to Tangier via Stockholm, and nobody wants that. And only the most promising airports get priced rather than every theoretically possible one, which keeps a search to a few seconds and keeps our traffic to the airline modest.

How fresh is the data?

Fares are re-checked every few hours, route maps every few days, the airport list monthly. Caching is why a repeat search returns instantly. It also means a price can be slightly stale — the airline's own page is always the truth.

Do I need a visa for the country I connect through?

Possibly, and we cannot tell you — it depends on your passport. What Flighthop can tell you is which country you will enter, and whether that crossing is one your trip already required. Both are on the trip detail. The rest is between you and that country's rules.

Is this run by Ryanair?

No. Not affiliated with them, not endorsed by them, not connected to them in any way. It is a personal tool that reads publicly available flight and fare information and does arithmetic on it.
